Temporal Dilation
The main characters in the Lost and Found series have enhanced Temporal Dilation (TD) abilities. Everyone has this ability to some extent or another. If, in a moment of danger, you have experienced time seeming to slow down, then you have experienced this same phenomenom.

Temporal Dilation is the brain's natural ability to adjust the subjective flow of time, stretching or compressing perception to match the demands of a situation. Everyone experiences it to some degree: moments can seem to slow in danger, or long, monotonous periods can pass in a flash. Most people slip into TD without realizing it, while those with enhanced TD enter it more easily and can maintain it with greater precision, allowing for extraordinarily fast reactions and heightened temporal acuity. Some individuals may never notice it under normal conditions, though the potential exists in most humans given the right circumstances.

TD can be measured indirectly through Critical Flicker Fusion Frequency (CFF), which gauges how quickly the brain perceives flickering light before it appears steady. Typical humans fall between 35-60 Hz, elite athletes like Simone Biles or Bruce Lee may reach 70-100 Hz under peak focus, and those with enhanced TD can exceed these levels, sometimes approaching hundreds of hertz. Elevated CFF is a measurable sign of the brain's accelerated temporal processing, though it is a symptom rather than the cause of TD.

Across species, TD exists on a spectrum. Slow animals like sloths register CFFs as low as 10-20 Hz, while extremely fast predators such as caracals or falcons can reach 100-150 Hz or higher. CFF decreases with age in humans. Humans with enhanced TD occupy the upper end of this continuum, able to react and perceive events at speeds approaching the fastest animals in nature.
