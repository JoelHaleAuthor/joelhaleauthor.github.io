Excerpt from Lost and Found: Family and Friends

We were driving into the late morning sun. Ronnie's bare feet were up on the passenger side of the dashboard. A crossword puzzle was on her thighs, and she was busy filling in the answers.

"An eight-letter word for a 'vocal amphibian'?" Ronnie was doing the crossword. I was the designated chauffeur.

"Gingrich," I suggested, "Newt Gingrich, he's Speaker of the House. Either that or 'bullfrog'."

"Very funny." She rolled her eyes, writing down one of my clever answers. "How about nine letters for 'Famous Bunny Prey'?"

"That would be the same answer as for 'Dismembered Emperor' – Bonaparte," I told her.

"I get 'bone apart,' but what's with the bunny?" she asked.

I was a wealth of useless facts. "Napoleon and his hunting party were supposedly attacked by a vicious horde of bunnies in 1807."

"Seriously?"

"Yeah. Apparently, Napoleon's chief of staff had arranged a rabbit hunt using 3000 or so tame bunnies. They hadn't been fed, and when released, they hungrily flanked Napoleon's party, hoping for food, probably. Napoleon and his group fled." I laughed. "Eight years before Waterloo, the Emperor of France was routed by bunnies. History's full of missed opportunities."

"Napoleon and the Duke of Wellington walk into a bar." Ronnie began another hilarious joke.

"Fewer shots this time? Or... Napoleon says, 'charge it?' Or... Wellington orders for both of them, a Waterloo Sunset and a Black Prussian?" My knowledge of mixed drinks came into play.

"The Duke says, 'This is awkward.' Napoleon says, 'at least you're not a bunny.'" I guess it was funny in context.

"'Religious figure involving bakers' - fourteen letters?" Ronnie went back to working on her crossword.

"Bread and Buddha?" I suggested, "Either that or Saint Elizabeth."

"Ha HA! Bread and Buddha? This is serious business!" Ronnie smiled at me. "Why Saint Elizabeth?"

"Have you forgotten we were good Catholic virgins in Georgetown?" I chided, "Saint Elizabeth is the patron saint of bakers."
