LEGACY AND HOPE is a propulsive, genre-blending novel that explores hidden histories, unconventional relationships, and the unseen forces shaping people's lives.

Allison Daws is a Los Angeles attorney with exceptional abilities and a close-knit, unconventional family. During an Outlander-inspired tour of the Scottish Highlands, she meets Jayce Pullman, a Department of Defense IT contractor. A romantic night in Inverness exposes Jayce to a virus with extraordinary consequences.

In California, Alli meets Evelyn, the mother of Jayce's eight-year-old daughter. Alli is drawn to Evelyn's beauty and to the secrets she seems determined to keep. As Alli's connection to Jayce and Evelyn deepens, she becomes entangled in a hidden scientific initiative linked to a homemade rag doll rescued from the Hull Blitz and to an organization devoted to preventing global catastrophes. Alli begins to suspect that her family's abilities may be connected to forces she has not considered.

The truth reaches back to 1971, when a man and a woman fled through the Congo rainforest to protect their unborn child from operatives of a covert research organization. Their escape ensured that the baby would survive, and that survival eventually led to the existence of Allison Daws and the extraordinary abilities she has always believed were simply part of who she is.

Legacy and Hope blends science, history, and romance across continents and generations. It follows Alli as she uncovers the legacy she never knew she inherited and the organization that has been shaping her life since before she was born.

Highland Tour, Part I, May 2026

Edinburgh, Sunday Night, 5/10

The lentil soup at the Lauriston Place Hotel restaurant was spicy and delicious. But when the most stunning creature I'd ever seen sat at a nearby table and ordered the same, I found myself ogling her for five full seconds before she caught me and said, "What?" She had caramel skin and spectacular hair.

"Sorry," I mumbled. I think I turned a striking shade of crimson.

That made her smile. She turned to her friend, a petite, attractive Asian woman. "Cindy, do I have spinach on my teeth or something?"

"No," Cindy replied, checking. Then more quietly, "Alli! He blushes like you."

"I thought I was the only one," she told her.

"Lord," I said quietly, "ye gave me a rare woman."

They both laughed.

"If we're going to spend the next two weeks on a bus together," Alli said, "you might as well join us."

"Je suis prêt," I said as I brought my soup over to their table.

"T'as vu Carfax Close, déjà ?" Alli asked me.

"Um," I said, trying to remember my college French. "Je vais aller le voir ce soir. Je crois que c'est en fait 'Bakehouse Close'." I'm going to see it tonight. I think it's actually called 'Bakehouse Close.'

Alli laughed, hopefully at my accent. "Maybe we should just speak English. I'm Alli, this is Cindy."

"I'm Jayce," I replied. "Short for JC."

This was not a bad start to a two-week Outlander-inspired tour of the Highlands. The first couple of days would be in Edinburgh. At least two of the busloads of people on the tour wouldn't be strangers. I had won this tour on some sort of raffle for defense contractors. I guess I was just lucky I was an Outlander fan.
