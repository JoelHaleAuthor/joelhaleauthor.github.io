LEAD AND FOLLOW is a genre-blending novel of reinvention, where a woman running from her past finds an unconventional family, a dance floor for her talents, and the nerve to choose her own path.


Samantha McClain has one suitcase, a bruised cheek, and a plan that fits in a single phrase: get to her brother in Los Angeles.

Three days later, she's knocking on what she believes is her brother's door. The woman who answers is very pregnant and not what Sam expected. Within a day, Sam has a new family, a new nickname for her ex ("Dick Witless"), and a new roommate, even more pregnant, and who radiates calm.

Sam has always been good at systems, code, and gymnastics. She has never been good at herself. Every man she dated made the decisions, and every time she told herself it wasn't that bad. Now, in a city where nobody knows her story, she decides to take control of her life.

She finds a new job and a swing dance floor that could change her life. She gets to know a family full of talent and fierce loyalty. Some of them are famous, some are dangerous, some are brilliant, and some are all three.

Sam is learning that she deserves better than what she had in Nebraska. But first she has to decide what that looks like.

Escape

January

By the early afternoon, Sam started seeing road signs for Los Angeles and San Diego. The mountains were pretty and she was living in the moment. It felt like meditating. She was aware of everything, heard the music and the time flew by. She felt good. Eventually she emerged in a flatter area. The Los Angeles area was known for its inland valleys. It wasn’t farmland, and it was occupied. Maybe this was one of those big valleys. Her GPS was telling her to take CA-210 to Pasadena. There were mountains to the right and open spaces to the left. She caught occasional glimpses of residential areas off to each side of the freeway as she merged onto CA-210.

After additional freeway changes she could see what she assumed was downtown Los Angeles to the right as she was heading west on I10 after a few more highway merges. A few miles farther, she took the Overland Avenue exit. She took a left then a right onto Rose Avenue. Rose Avenue looked like Apartment Row with an endless collection of residential apartments. Sam found a convenient spot in front of Marcus’s complex to park her Civic. It was 2:45 p.m. Marcus might be at Culver City Gymnastics now, but she figured she’d try here first. She put her keys in her shoulder bag after exiting the car.

The apartment was upstairs facing the street. Sam knocked on the door. From inside she heard a feminine voice, slightly irritated, “Seriously? Right when I have to pee? You’d better not be the plumber!”

“Not the plumber,” Sam called out. “I'm looking for Marcus.”

The door opened. An impatient, extremely pregnant white woman with blond hair was standing there wearing nothing but some plaid boxer shorts. Sam tried to look directly into her eyes. “You aren’t one of his mistresses, are you?” the woman asked, one hand on her hip.

“Um, no,” Sam replied. “I'm his sister.”

“Oh!” the woman said. “You must be Sam! I'd hug you, but I'd probably pee on you.” She turned toward the interior of the apartment. “Come on in, close the door.”
